# Details sheet

Every detail shown for each piece and composer (year, place, instruments, how it was received,
the composer's age, money and background), with where it can be checked. Only documented facts are
used. Where a source is uncertain the wording says so ("c.", "about", "probably"), and where nothing
is documented it says "Not documented".

Age at writing = `composedYear` − composer's birth year, less one when the piece was finished before
that year's birthday (Satie, Schumann, Beethoven, Saint-Saëns). "About" marks cases where the birthday or a
date range makes it uncertain.

## Pieces

### 1. Gymnopédie No. 1 · Erik Satie

- **Year:** 1888 · `composedYear: 1888`
- **Age:** about 21. The set was finished by 2 April 1888, before his 22nd birthday on 17 May.
- **Place:** Paris (Montmartre), France
- **Instruments:** Solo piano
- **Reception:** It got little notice at first. Public attention came after Debussy orchestrated Nos. 1 and 3 in 1897.

Sources: [Gymnopédies (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnop%C3%A9dies),
[Erik Satie (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Satie)

### 2. Clair de lune (Suite bergamasque) · Claude Debussy

- **Year:** c. 1890 (revised and published 1905) · `composedYear: 1890`
- **Age:** about 28 (Wikipedia: "around 1890, at the age of 28")
- **Place:** probably Paris, France
- **Instruments:** Solo piano
- **Reception:** Debussy did not want to publish these early pieces. He agreed in 1905 only because a publisher expected his later fame to sell them.

Sources: [Suite bergamasque (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suite_bergamasque),
[Claude Debussy (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Debussy)

### 3. Goldberg Variations: Aria · J. S. Bach

- **Year:** c. 1740 (published 1741) · `composedYear: 1740`
- **Age:** about 55
- **Place:** Leipzig, Germany
- **Instruments:** Harpsichord with two manuals
- **Reception:** It was one of the few works Bach published in his lifetime. It became widely known only after Glenn Gould's 1955 recording.

Sources: [Goldberg Variations (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldberg_Variations)

### 4. Gnossienne No. 1 · Erik Satie

- **Year:** c. 1890 (published 1893) · `composedYear: 1890`
- **Age:** about 24
- **Place:** Paris (Montmartre), France
- **Instruments:** Solo piano (no time signature or bar lines)
- **Reception:** How it was received is not documented. It first appeared in the magazine *Le Figaro musical* in September 1893.

Sources: [Gnossiennes (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnossiennes),
[Erik Satie (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Satie)

### 5. Nocturne in E-flat major, Op. 9 No. 2 · Frédéric Chopin

- **Year:** 1830–32 (published 1832) · `composedYear: 1830`
- **Age:** about 20
- **Place:** uncertain: written while Chopin moved from Warsaw to Vienna (Nov. 1830) and then Paris (Oct. 1831)
- **Instruments:** Solo piano
- **Reception:** Critics gave Chopin's nocturnes a mixed reception when they first came out. Today Op. 9 No. 2 is often called his most famous piece.

Sources: [Nocturnes, Op. 9 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnes,_Op._9_(Chopin)),
[Nocturnes (Chopin) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnes_(Chopin)),
[IMSLP: Nocturnes, Op. 9](https://imslp.org/wiki/Nocturnes,_Op.9_(Chopin,_Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric))

### 6. Cello Suite No. 1 in G major: Prelude · J. S. Bach

- **Year:** c. 1717–23 · `composedYear: 1720` (middle of the range)
- **Age:** about 35 (32 to 38 across the range)
- **Place:** probably Köthen, Germany
- **Instruments:** Solo cello
- **Reception:** The suites stayed largely unknown until Pablo Casals found an edition in Barcelona in 1889. His 1936–39 recordings made them famous.

Sources: [Cello Suites (Bach) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cello_Suites_(Bach))

### 7. Träumerei (Kinderszenen, Op. 15) · Robert Schumann

- **Year:** 1838 · `composedYear: 1838`
- **Age:** 27. It was written in February–March 1838, before his 28th birthday on 8 June.
- **Place:** Leipzig, Germany
- **Instruments:** Solo piano
- **Reception:** Clara Wieck, who married Schumann in 1840, loved the set. How the wider public first received it is less well documented, so nothing more is claimed here.

Sources: [Kinderszenen (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinderszenen),
[IMSLP: Kinderszenen](https://imslp.org/wiki/Kinderszenen,_Op.15_(Schumann,_Robert)),
[Kinderszenen notes (Uppsala Univ. page)](https://user.it.uu.se/~joachim/Kinderszenen.htm)

### 8. Prelude in C major, BWV 846 (Well-Tempered Clavier, Book 1) · J. S. Bach

- **Year:** c. 1720–22 (printed 1801) · `composedYear: 1720`
- **Age:** about 35
- **Place:** Köthen, Germany
- **Instruments:** Keyboard (harpsichord or clavichord)
- **Reception:** It circulated in handwritten copies during Bach's life and was not printed until 1801, 51 years after his death. Gounod later used it for his "Ave Maria".

Sources: [The Well-Tempered Clavier (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Well-Tempered_Clavier),
[Prelude and Fugue in C major, BWV 846 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prelude_and_Fugue_in_C_major,_BWV_846)

### 9. Peer Gynt: Morning Mood · Edvard Grieg

- **Year:** 1874–75 (premiered 1876; in Suite No. 1, 1888) · `composedYear: 1874`
- **Age:** about 31
- **Place:** Bergen, Norway (begun at Sandviken). It was finished in Fredensborg, Denmark, in July 1875.
- **Instruments:** Orchestra (flutes, oboes, clarinets, bassoons, horns, trumpets, timpani, strings)
- **Reception:** The 1876 premiere in Christiania (Oslo) was a "triumphant success". The suites became some of Grieg's best-known works.

Sources: [Peer Gynt (Grieg) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_Gynt_(Grieg)),
[Morning Mood (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Mood),
[Bergen Public Library: Grieg biography](https://bergenbibliotek.no/grieg/english/bio2-eng)

### 10. Piano Sonata No. 16 in C major, K. 545: Allegro · W. A. Mozart

- **Year:** 1788 (published 1805) · `composedYear: 1788`
- **Age:** 32
- **Place:** Vienna, Austria
- **Instruments:** Solo piano
- **Reception:** Mozart called it "a little piano sonata for beginners". It was not published in his lifetime.

Sources: [Piano Sonata No. 16 (Mozart) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._16_(Mozart)),
[Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)

### 11. Air on the G String (Orchestral Suite No. 3, BWV 1068) · J. S. Bach

- **Year:** c. 1730 · `composedYear: 1730`
- **Age:** about 45
- **Place:** Leipzig, Germany (earliest surviving parts). Some scholars think the suite started earlier, in Köthen.
- **Instruments:** Strings and continuo
- **Reception:** How it was received in Bach's time is not documented. It became famous through August Wilhelmj's 1871 violin arrangement, which gave it the "G string" name.

Sources: [Orchestral suites (Bach) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestral_suites_(Bach)),
[Air on the G String (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_on_the_G_String)

### 12. Moonlight Sonata (Op. 27 No. 2): Adagio sostenuto · Ludwig van Beethoven

- **Year:** 1801 (published 1802) · `composedYear: 1801`
- **Age:** about 30 (born December 1770)
- **Place:** Vienna, Austria
- **Instruments:** Solo piano ("harpsichord or pianoforte" on the title page)
- **Reception:** It was popular in his own day. Czerny reported Beethoven grumbling, "Surely I've written better things."

Sources: [Piano Sonata No. 14 (Beethoven) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Sonata_No._14_(Beethoven))

### 13. The Carnival of the Animals: The Swan · Camille Saint-Saëns

- **Year:** 1886 (Swan published 1887; full suite 1922) · `composedYear: 1886`
- **Age:** 50
- **Place:** a small village in Austria (Wikipedia does not name it)
- **Instruments:** Cello and two pianos
- **Reception:** Saint-Saëns banned publication of the Carnival in his lifetime, except The Swan, which he allowed in 1887.

Sources: [The Carnival of the Animals (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Carnival_of_the_Animals)

### 14. Venetian Gondola Song, Op. 30 No. 6 · Felix Mendelssohn

- **Year:** 1833–34 (published 1835) · `composedYear: 1833`
- **Age:** about 24
- **Place:** probably Düsseldorf, Germany, where he was music director from 1833 to 1835
- **Instruments:** Solo piano
- **Reception:** The *Songs Without Words* were widely popular with amateur pianists, and he went on to publish eight books of them. Reception of this single piece is not documented.

Sources: [Songs Without Words (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_Without_Words),
[IMSLP: Lieder ohne Worte, Op. 30](https://imslp.org/wiki/Lieder_ohne_Worte,_Op.30_(Mendelssohn,_Felix)),
[Interlude: Songs without Words](https://interlude.hk/the-lyrical-piano-mendelssohn-lieder-ohne-worte-songs-without-words/)

### 15. Nocturne in E minor, Op. 72 No. 1 · Frédéric Chopin

- **Year:** 1827 (published 1855) · `composedYear: 1827`
- **Age:** about 17
- **Place:** Warsaw, Poland, where he lived and studied at the time
- **Instruments:** Solo piano
- **Reception:** Published only after his death, by Julian Fontana, so it had no audience in his lifetime. It was his first nocturne.

Sources: [Nocturne in E minor, Op. posth. 72 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturne_in_E_minor,_Op._posth._72_(Chopin)),
[Nocturnes (Chopin) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnes_(Chopin))

## Composers

### Erik Satie

- **Born:** 1866, Honfleur, France · **Died:** 1925
- **Money:** He earned a modest living as a cabaret pianist, was often short of money, and from 1898 lived in one room in Arcueil.
- **Also:** He was expelled from the Paris Conservatoire in 1882. He went back to study at the Schola Cantorum at 39.

Sources: [Erik Satie (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Satie)

### Claude Debussy

- **Born:** 1862, Saint-Germain-en-Laye, France · **Died:** 1918
- **Money:** He had "a taste for luxury" and a reputation for debts, and supplemented his income by teaching and writing.
- **Also:** He entered the Paris Conservatoire at ten and won the Prix de Rome in 1884.

Sources: [Claude Debussy (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Debussy)

### Johann Sebastian Bach

- **Born:** 1685, Eisenach, Germany · **Died:** 1750
- **Money:** He was a salaried musician all his life, as court Kapellmeister at Köthen ("paid him well") and then Thomaskantor in Leipzig.
- **Also:** He had 20 children, and four became composers.

Sources: [Johann Sebastian Bach (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach)

### Frédéric Chopin

- **Born:** 1810, Żelazowa Wola, Poland · **Died:** 1849
- **Money:** He earned "a handsome income" from publishing and from teaching wealthy pupils, and gave few public concerts.
- **Also:** He left Poland in 1830 and never returned. He settled in Paris.

Sources: [Frédéric Chopin (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Chopin)

### Robert Schumann

- **Born:** 1810, Zwickau, Germany · **Died:** 1856
- **Money:** Clara's father opposed their marriage, fearing Schumann could not provide for her. She was the better-known performer of the two.
- **Also:** An injured finger ended his hopes of being a concert pianist, so he turned to composing.

Sources: [Robert Schumann (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Schumann)

### Edvard Grieg

- **Born:** 1843, Bergen, Norway · **Died:** 1907
- **Money:** From 1874 he received an annual artist's stipend from the Norwegian state.
- **Also:** He trained at the Leipzig Conservatory.

Sources: [Edvard Grieg (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Grieg),
[Bergen Public Library: Grieg biography](https://bergenbibliotek.no/grieg/english/bio2-eng)

### Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

- **Born:** 1756, Salzburg (now Austria) · **Died:** 1791
- **Money:** His income fell sharply by 1788, and he wrote begging letters for loans to his friend Michael Puchberg.
- **Also:** As a child prodigy he toured Europe's courts for three and a half years.

Sources: [Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)

### Ludwig van Beethoven

- **Born:** 1770, Bonn, Germany · **Died:** 1827
- **Money:** From 1809 three noble patrons promised him 4,000 florins a year to stay in Vienna, but not all of them kept paying.
- **Also:** His hearing began to fail around 1798.

Sources: [Ludwig van Beethoven (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven)

### Camille Saint-Saëns

- **Born:** 1835, Paris, France · **Died:** 1921
- **Money:** Being organist at La Madeleine gave him a comfortable income. Later a legacy let him concentrate on composing.
- **Also:** He was a famous child prodigy and had perfect pitch before he was three.

Sources: [Camille Saint-Saëns (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Saint-Sa%C3%ABns)

### Felix Mendelssohn

- **Born:** 1809, Hamburg, Germany · **Died:** 1847
- **Money:** He came from a wealthy family. His father Abraham was a banker.
- **Also:** In 1829 he conducted Bach's St Matthew Passion in Berlin, which sparked the Bach revival.

Sources: [Felix Mendelssohn (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Mendelssohn)
